## a few projects

<details>
<summary>airfoil research</summary>

<a href="https://github.com/eshantsonhar/Airfoil-Generator-Model">Airfoil Generator & Aerodynamic Optimization</a>

A computational aerodynamics project exploring whether low-Reynolds-number airfoils can be improved through geometry alone.

The project uses Class-Shape Transformation to parameterize airfoil geometry, SU2 for CFD simulations, and gradient-based optimization to modify the shape while maintaining aerodynamic and structural constraints.

The goal is to reduce laminar separation bubbles and improve aerodynamic efficiency without relying on physical turbulators or other active flow-control devices.

At one test condition, the optimization reduced profile drag by 40.8% and increased the lift-to-drag ratio by 31.3%.

</details>

<summary>hardware & physical experiments</summary>

A collection of projects where the interesting part is making something actually exist outside a screen.

I've worked with microcontrollers, Raspberry Pis, sensors, motors, high-voltage circuits, 3D printing, and custom electronics. One of the more ridiculous experiments has been working with Tesla-coil circuitry, exploring resonant high-voltage systems and trying to understand what is happening electrically rather than just making sparks and calling it science.

I've also experimented with building my own mechanical systems and electronics, including a DIY keyboard, custom components, sensor setups, and various small embedded projects.

A lot of the work sits at the intersection of electronics, mechanical design and software. CAD experiments in Autodesk Fusion are usually somewhere in the middle of that process, turning an idea into something that can actually be fabricated.

</details>

<summary>systems, linux & self-hosting</summary>

I like having computers that do things without needing someone else's infrastructure to do them.

I've spent a fair amount of time working with Linux, Raspberry Pi systems, SSH, networking, storage, services, systemd, shell scripting and self-hosted software.

One of my Raspberry Pi projects turned a Pi into a small home server, including external storage and a self-hosted music server. I've also experimented with file sharing, remote administration, headless systems, service management and running software continuously on relatively constrained hardware.

On the desktop side, I've spent far too much time reorganizing installations, moving applications and data between drives, managing Windows/Linux environments, fixing broken paths and making computers do exactly what I want them to do.
